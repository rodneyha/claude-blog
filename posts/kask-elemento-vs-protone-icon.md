# KASK Elemento vs KASK Protone Icon: Aerodynamics, Weight, and Value Compared (2026)

> **Key Takeaways**
> - The KASK Elemento saves approximately 5 watts at 45 km/h and earns a rare 5-star Virginia Tech safety rating — but it costs $100 more and weighs 32–50 g more than the Protone Icon ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/reviews/helmets/road-cycling-helmets/kask-elemento-helmet-review), 2024).
> - The Protone Icon's 20 ventilation openings make it measurably better for hot-weather road riding and long climbs.
> - Choose the Elemento for racing and gravel. Choose the Protone Icon if weight, ventilation, or value are your priority.

KASK's two flagship road helmets aren't really competing with each other — they're built for different riders. The Protone Icon ($299.95) has been the WorldTour standard since Team Sky debuted it at the 2014 Tour de France, a decade of race wins and proven aerodynamic performance behind it. The Elemento ($400), launched in 2023, pushed the technology further with a Fluid Carbon 12 shell, 3D-printed Multipod padding, and Virginia Tech's highest independent safety tier.

So which one should you buy? That depends on whether you race or ride — and how much you care about that $100 gap. This comparison breaks down aerodynamics, ventilation, safety, fit, weight, and value so you can make a confident call.

## Which Helmet Is More Aerodynamic?

**The Elemento wins on aerodynamics** — KASK's wind tunnel data shows a 1.5% reduction in drag versus the Protone Icon, translating to approximately 5 watts saved at 45 km/h in a road position ([KASK / Cycling Weekly](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/reviews/kask-elemento-review-its-the-most-expensive-helmet-weve-had-on-test), 2024). At race pace, that's a meaningful advantage; at typical training speeds, it's close to negligible.

The Elemento's Fluid Carbon 12 shell — 30% carbon fiber bonded with 70% reinforced plastic in an injection-molded process — allows thinner, more aerodynamically sculpted panels than standard polycarbonate can achieve ([Cyclingnews](https://www.cyclingnews.com/news/kask-launches-elemento-helmet-with-carbon-fibre-plates-and-3d-printed-pads/), 2023). KASK used CFD simulation, thermal modelling, and wind tunnel testing during development to optimize airflow across the surface.

The Protone Icon isn't slow. Its shape has been refined over a decade of WorldTour racing and it remains competitive against other aero road helmets at its price point. At speeds below 35 km/h, the gap between the two helmets shrinks to the point where most riders won't feel a difference.

> **Our finding:** The Elemento's aero gains apply at race pace (40–50 km/h) in an upright road position. Below 35 km/h or on tri-bars, the Protone Icon gives up almost nothing.

**Verdict: Elemento wins on aerodynamics — meaningfully so at speeds above 38–40 km/h in road position.**

## Which Helmet Keeps You Cooler?

**The Protone Icon wins on ventilation** — its 20 large external openings consistently move more air across the head at lower speeds and during climbing efforts where airspeed drops ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/reviews/helmets/road-cycling-helmets/kask-protone-icon-helmet-review), 2024). Multiple independent reviewers rate it as one of the better-ventilated helmets in the $300 price band.

KASK claims the Elemento actually keeps your head 0.26°C cooler than the Protone Icon through its larger internal channels and air-permeable Multipod pads ([KASK official](https://www.kask.com/en-us/Elemento/CHE00101-.html), 2023). That figure comes from KASK's own thermal simulations at sustained high speeds. At slower paces — where most of us actually ride — reviewer consensus leans toward the Protone Icon as the cooler helmet.

The two helmets also differ in sweat management. The Protone Icon's Coolmax liner absorbs moisture and is removable for washing — a practical advantage on multi-hour rides. The Elemento's Multipod pads don't absorb sweat, which means no soaked foam after a hard effort, but it does mean sweat drips rather than wicks. Both approaches have their supporters.

According to KASK's thermal documentation, the Elemento's internal airflow design maintains a lower average head temperature during sustained high-speed efforts — the 0.26°C advantage cited in their 2023 product documentation. Whether that margin holds at club-ride pace is a separate question that independent testers haven't confirmed.

**Verdict: Protone Icon wins for ventilation on hot days, long climbs, and lower-intensity rides. The Elemento's cooling advantage, if real, applies at race pace only.**

## Which Helmet Is Safer?

**The Elemento wins on safety certifications** — it's one of the few road helmets to earn a 5-star rating from Virginia Tech's independent Helmet Lab, the most rigorous third-party cycling helmet test available ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/reviews/helmets/road-cycling-helmets/kask-elemento-helmet-review), 2024). Both helmets pass CPSC (US standard) and WG11 (KASK's rotational impact protocol), but Virginia Tech's 5-star designation places the Elemento in a genuinely elite safety tier.

Virginia Tech's STAR methodology evaluates helmets across 48 impact conditions — linear and rotational — at multiple energy levels. A 5-star outcome means the helmet ranks among the best available for concussion risk reduction. It's not a marketing label; it's an independently funded, peer-reviewed test. Not every expensive helmet earns it.

The Fluid Carbon 12 shell contributes directly to this result. Carbon fiber distributes impact energy across a wider surface area than standard polycarbonate, reducing peak forces at the contact point. The 3D-printed Multipod polymer lattice pads also manage multiple impact vectors differently from foam — foam compresses once; the lattice structure is designed to perform repeatedly.

Both helmets carry KASK's WG11 certification — KASK's own rotational impact standard, which KASK claims is more demanding than the baseline EN 1078 test for rotational forces. The Protone Icon is a safe helmet with a long racing record. It simply hasn't earned a 5-star Virginia Tech rating.

**Verdict: Elemento wins on safety. The Virginia Tech 5-star rating is independent and rigorous — a meaningful edge over WG11-only certification.**

## Which Helmet Fits Better?

**The Elemento wins on fit adjustability** — its OCTOFIT+ system adds a vertical (UP&DOWN) adjustment axis that the standard OCTOFIT on the Protone Icon doesn't offer ([KASK official](https://www.kask.com/en-us/Elemento/CHE00101-.html), 2023). For riders whose head shape doesn't align cleanly with a round-oval shell profile, that extra dimension eliminates pressure points over long rides.

The standard OCTOFIT retention ring on the Protone Icon adjusts horizontally — secure, proven, and adequate for most head shapes. It's the system that's served INEOS Grenadiers riders through a decade of Grand Tour racing. Reviewers describe it as reliable, though some note it feels slightly less refined than the OCTOFIT+ given the Elemento's higher price point.

The Multipod pads change the fit feel as well. They don't compress the same way foam does — some riders prefer the "floating" feel with direct airflow to the scalp; others find it less secure than traditional foam. This is personal and worth testing in a shop if you can.

**Verdict: Elemento wins on fit for riders who need vertical adjustment. The Protone Icon's OCTOFIT is proven and sufficient for most head shapes.**

## Which Helmet Is Lighter?

**The Protone Icon wins on weight** — BikeRadar independently measured it at 239 g (size M) versus 271 g for the Elemento, a 32 g difference ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/reviews/helmets/road-cycling-helmets/kask-elemento-helmet-review), 2024). KASK's claimed weights are 220 g and 260 g respectively — both lighter than BikeRadar's scale, which is standard for manufacturer specs. Either way, the Protone Icon is consistently 32–50 g lighter depending on the source.

Thirty-two grams is roughly a gel packet. On flat roads at 40 km/h, it doesn't matter. On a 2,000 m alpine climb at threshold, it's a real but small factor — the power difference from a 32 g helmet change is a fraction of a watt. The Protone Icon's weight advantage is more noticeable as comfort over a long day than as measurable speed.

The Fluid Carbon 12 shell is stiff, thin, and aerodynamic — but carbon composite construction isn't a route to minimum weight when you're also trying to achieve a 5-star safety rating. KASK's trade-off is explicit: safety and aerodynamics come first; weight savings is secondary.

For road racing and gravel use, the Elemento ($400 USD) costs $100.05 more than the Protone Icon ($299.95 USD) — a 33% premium for a helmet that's heavier and has fewer ventilation openings, but delivers superior aerodynamics, an independent 5-star safety rating, and a more adjustable fit system ([KASK official](https://www.kask.com/en-us/Elemento/CHE00101-.html), 2024).

The value question hinges on how you ride. If you race, the 5W aero advantage and Virginia Tech 5-star certification are concrete returns on that extra $100. If you train, ride sportives, or prioritize long-day comfort, the Protone Icon delivers 90% of the performance for 75% of the price.

There's a long-term cost angle too. The Elemento's Multipod pads have drawn early reports of delamination from some reviewers. The Protone Icon's Coolmax liner has a decade of proven durability and is easily replaced. That's not a reason to avoid the Elemento, but it's worth noting.

**Value verdict: Protone Icon wins on value for most riders. The Elemento's premium is justified for racers and riders who specifically want the Virginia Tech 5-star rating.**

## Who Should Choose Which Helmet?

If you race crits, road races, or gravel events — or want the highest independently verified safety rating — choose the Elemento. If ventilation, weight, or value are your priorities, the Protone Icon is the smarter buy for most riders.

**The racer or competitive cyclist:** Choose the Elemento. The 5W aero advantage at race pace is real, and the Virginia Tech 5-star rating adds genuine confidence in a crash. INEOS Grenadiers use it as their primary race helmet.

**The gran fondo or sportive rider:** Choose the Protone Icon. You'll spend 4–6 hours on the bike, often at lower speeds where the Elemento's aero gains disappear. The 20 vents keep you cooler on long climbs, and 32 g less on your head is noticeable over a big day.

**The climber:** Choose the Protone Icon. Every gram counts on sustained ascents, and ventilation performance at 15–25 km/h makes a comfort difference that the Elemento can't match at those speeds.

**The gravel or multi-discipline rider:** Choose the Elemento. KASK specifically designed it for road, gravel, MTB XC, and cyclocross. The OCTOFIT+ handles the varied head positions those disciplines demand, and the safety rating holds regardless of surface.

**The training-focused cyclist:** Choose the Protone Icon. It's not a compromise — it's a decade-tested WorldTour helmet. At $299.95, you're not settling for second best.

If neither fits your use case — if you need pure time-trial aerodynamics — look at the KASK Utopia Y, KASK's dedicated TT helmet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the KASK Elemento better than the Protone Icon?

It depends on your riding. The Elemento wins on aerodynamics (approximately 5 watts saved at 45 km/h), safety (5-star Virginia Tech), and fit adjustability (OCTOFIT+). The Protone Icon wins on weight (32–50 g lighter), ventilation (20 openings), and value ($100 less). For racing, the Elemento. For training, gran fondos, and hot-weather rides, the Protone Icon is the stronger choice for most cyclists.

### How much does the KASK Elemento weigh compared to the Protone Icon?

BikeRadar independently measured the Elemento at 271 g and the Protone Icon at 239 g (both size M) — a 32 g difference ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/reviews/helmets/road-cycling-helmets/kask-elemento-helmet-review), 2024). KASK's claimed weights are 260 g and 220 g respectively, slightly lighter than independent measurements, which is standard practice for manufacturer specifications.

### Does the KASK Elemento actually save watts?

KASK claims approximately 5 watts saved at 45 km/h versus the Protone Icon in wind tunnel testing using a full-body mannequin ([KASK / Cycling Weekly](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/reviews/kask-elemento-review-its-the-most-expensive-helmet-weve-had-on-test), 2024). Independent testing by 220 Triathlon found the Elemento 3.4 seconds faster over a 40 km leg at 30 km/h in road position — but 5 seconds slower on tri-bars at 40 km/h. The gains are real but speed- and position-dependent.

### What is the Fluid Carbon 12 shell in the KASK Elemento?

Fluid Carbon 12 is KASK's composite material: 30% carbon fiber bonded with 70% reinforced plastic in an injection-molded process ([Cyclingnews](https://www.cyclingnews.com/news/kask-launches-elemento-helmet-with-carbon-fibre-plates-and-3d-printed-pads/), 2023). It enables thinner, aerodynamically precise panels while distributing impact energy more evenly than standard polycarbonate — a key factor in the helmet's Virginia Tech 5-star safety rating.

### Is the KASK Protone Icon still worth buying in 2026?

Yes. The Protone Icon is one of the best all-round road helmets available at its price point. It's been refined since its 2014 Tour de France debut with Team Sky, holds WG11 rotational impact certification, and offers excellent ventilation. At $299.95 / £245, it delivers strong value and remains the better choice for the majority of cyclists who prioritize comfort, cooling, and value over peak aerodynamic performance.
